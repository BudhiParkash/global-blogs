These three rules make the content feel authored, not generated.

RULE 1 — FIRST-PERSON EXPERIENCE LINES
Include at least one first-person experience line per H2 section.
Use phrases like:
  "In our experience testing these..."
  "We found that..."
  "When we looked at this across multiple options..."
  "What surprised us was..."
  "After going through dozens of options, we noticed..."
Never fabricate specific personal events — keep it editorial "we"
as in a publication testing and reviewing products or destinations.

RULE 2 — SENTENCE RHYTHM VARIATION
Vary sentence length deliberately in every paragraph.
Mix short punchy sentences (4–7 words) with longer ones (18–25 words).
Never write three sentences of similar length in a row.
Example of good rhythm:
  "Most people get this wrong. They spend hours comparing specs when the
  single most important factor — the one that actually determines whether
  you will be happy with your choice six months from now — is something
  almost nobody talks about."

RULE 3 — STRONG NAMED OPINIONS
Every post must contain at least one section where we take a clear,
specific stance. Not "some people prefer X" but "X is the wrong choice
for most Indians and here is exactly why."
This signals a real editorial voice to both readers and Google's
Helpful Content system.
